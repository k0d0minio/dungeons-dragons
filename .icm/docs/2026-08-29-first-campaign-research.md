## 1. Rules edition: the 2024 rules

The revised 5e (marketed simply as "Dungeons & Dragons"; "One D&D" was only the
playtest name) is the current standard: the 2025 starter set and all new print
material use it, the CC-licensed SRD 5.2 encodes it, and 2014-era adventures remain
compatible. There is no strong reason for a brand-new 2026 group to learn 2014 rules
first.

Differences that affect character creation and the data model:

- **Background is the mechanical origin**: each background grants +2/+1 (or
  +1/+1/+1) among three fixed abilities, an Origin feat, two skills, and a tool.
  Species ("race" is retired as a term) grants traits only — no ability scores.
  Half-elf and half-orc no longer exist as options.
- **Weapon Mastery**: martials unlock one of eight per-weapon properties — Cleave,
  Graze, Nick, Push, Sap, Slow, Topple, Vex — a new subsystem the sheet should
  surface.
- **All classes get their subclass at level 3** (uniform; no level-1 Cleric domains
  or level-2 Wizard schools).
- **Tidied action list** (Attack, Dash, Disengage, Dodge, Help, Hide, Influence,
  Magic, Ready, Search, Study, Utilize), **only one spell-slot-expending spell per
  turn**, simpler exhaustion (cumulative −2 to d20 tests), surprise = disadvantage
  on initiative, "Heroic Inspiration".

Change-logs: [RPGBot transition guide](https://rpgbot.net/dnd-2024-5e-transition-guide-and-change-log-everything-thats-different-in-the-new-players-handbook/) ·
[Forst Stories comparison](https://forststories.com/dd-5e-vs-dd-2024/)

## 2. Licensing: SRD 5.2.1 under CC-BY-4.0

SRD 5.2 (April 2025; current revision **5.2.1**) puts the 2024 core rules under
**Creative Commons Attribution 4.0** — irrevocable, commercial use allowed, so a
free friends-only app is comfortably inside it *for SRD text*. Official PDF:
[SRD_CC_v5.2](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/srd/5.2/SRD_CC_v5.2.pdf) ·
landing: [dndbeyond.com/srd](https://www.dndbeyond.com/srd)

**What's in:**

- **All 12 classes, exactly one subclass each**: Berserker Barbarian, College of
  Lore Bard, Life Domain Cleric, Circle of the Land Druid, Champion Fighter,
  Warrior of the Open Hand Monk, Oath of Devotion Paladin, Hunter Ranger, Thief
  Rogue, Draconic Sorcerer, Fiend Patron Warlock, Evoker Wizard. No Artificer.
- **9 species**: Dragonborn, Dwarf, Elf, Gnome, Goliath, Halfling, Human, Orc,
  Tiefling. No Aasimar.
- **4 backgrounds**: Acolyte, Criminal, Sage, Soldier.
- **16 feats** (Alert, Magic Initiate, Savage Attacker, Skilled, ASI,
  fighting-style feats) plus Epic Boons; ~400 spells; 300+ monsters from the 2025
  Monster Manual; a large magic-item list.

**What's out (WotC-proprietary):** all other PHB subclasses, Aasimar, expansion
content (Volo's / Monsters of the Multiverse species and monsters), the classic
product-identity monsters (beholder, mind flayer, displacer beast, githyanki,
kuo-toa, slaadi, umber hulk, yuan-ti), named beings and settings (Strahd, Tiamat,
Forgotten Realms), and trademarked item names (SRD renames: Deck of Many Things →
"Mysterious Deck").

**Attribution** (verbatim, footer/about page): *"This work includes material from
the System Reference Document 5.2.1 ('SRD 5.2.1') by Wizards of the Coast LLC,
available at https://www.dndbeyond.com/srd. The SRD 5.2.1 is licensed under the
Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License."*

**Pitfalls:** "free and private" is not a legal defence — copying non-SRD rules
text (other subclasses, starter-set adventure text) into shipped app data is
infringement regardless of price. CC covers the *text*, not trademarks — no D&D
logo, no implied endorsement. The safe pattern: ship SRD content as data; the DM
hand-enters anything from books he owns as private freeform notes. Don't scrape
D&D Beyond.

## 3. What beginners struggle with

([EN World thread](https://www.enworld.org/threads/what-have-you-found-to-be-the-hardest-thing-for-new-players-to-understand-about-5e-systems.635539/) ·
[TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/dungeons-dragons-dnd-mistakes-first-time-players/))

- **Action economy** — what one turn allows (action + bonus action + movement +
  one reaction off-turn); that bonus actions aren't "spare actions".
- **Spellcasting bookkeeping** — known vs prepared vs slots; cantrips are
  unlimited; upcasting; concentration (one spell at a time).
- **What to roll when** — attack roll vs ability check vs saving throw, which
  modifier, when proficiency applies. The single highest-value app feature: "tap
  the thing, see the right roll and modifier".
- **Reading the sheet** — where AC / spell DC / initiative come from; forgetting
  class features exist.

Choices that actually change moment-to-moment play: **class** above all (caster =
high cognitive load; Champion Fighter / Thief Rogue = low — steer hesitant players
there), chosen **cantrips/spells**, **weapon** (mastery property), the background's
**Origin feat**, and skill proficiencies. Species and flavour choices barely affect
play.

**Session zero** covers: campaign pitch/tone, table expectations, safety tools,
house rules, and making characters *together* with party ties
([checklist](https://saga20.com/blog/dnd-session-zero-checklist/)).

## 4. First campaign for a new DM

**Recommended: *Heroes of the Borderlands* (Sept 2025 starter set)** — first set on
2024 rules, levels 1–3, episodic, offloads bookkeeping with cards/boards, teaches
the DM as it goes ([The Alexandrian review](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/52962/roleplaying-games/review-heroes-of-the-borderlands-dd-starter-set-2025) ·
[Geek to Geek](https://geektogeekmedia.com/geekery/tabletop-gaming/dnd-5e-heroes-of-the-borderlands-starter-set-review/)).
*Lost Mine of Phandelver* (2014) is beloved but on old rules; *Dragons of
Stormwreck Isle* (2022) is the weak option.

Session-1 prep (per the [Lazy DM eight steps](https://slyflourish.com/eight_steps_2023.html)):
review characters, a strong start, ~3–5 potential scenes, ~10 one-sentence
secrets/clues, key locations, NPC names + motivations, monster stat blocks,
treasure. At the table: initiative, monster HP, DM-only info kept apart from
player-facing info.

**Where a tool genuinely helps**: initiative + monster HP (shipped), stat-block /
spell / condition lookup, encounter-difficulty math, NPC roster with secret notes,
secret-info gating, session log/recap. What it can't help: pacing, rulings,
improvisation — don't try.

## 5. Prior art

- **D&D Beyond** (the benchmark): three creation modes — *Standard* (step-by-step
  wizard, optional help text), *Quick Build*, *Premade*. In play: tap-to-roll with
  modifiers pre-computed, every term a popover.
  ([creation methods](https://dndbeyond-support.wizards.com/hc/en-us/articles/7747193997716-Character-Creation-Methods))
- **Roll20**: the cautionary tale — menus-within-menus; novices drown. Its
  Charactermancer wizard is the good part.
- **Patterns that work**: linear wizard with a recommended default pre-selected at
  every step; progressive disclosure (hide upcasting/feats-vs-ASI until relevant);
  contextual popovers instead of rulebook links; quick-build for session 1, full
  builder later.
- **What overwhelms**: unfiltered option lists (even SRD 5.2's ~400 spells);
  toggle sprawl; raw stat blocks; jargon without inline definitions; flavour
  decisions before mechanical ones.

**Design implication**: SRD 5.2's "one subclass per class" limit is a *feature*
for four-to-six complete beginners — 12 classes × 1 subclass × 9 species × 4
backgrounds is a perfectly sized decision space, fully legal to encode, with
everything else living in DM-entered notes.
