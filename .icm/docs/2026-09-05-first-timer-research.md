## 1. First session experience for brand-new players

- **Minimum before the first turn is one sentence, not a rules talk.** The Angry GM's reverse-satire boils it down to "describe your action and we'll see what happens", with "simplified pregenerated characters" and rules introduced as they come up; explaining dice, ability scores and paperwork first is the anti-pattern. [Angry GM](https://theangrygm.com/how-not-to-teach-newbies-dd/)
- **The 2025 starter box budgets ten minutes.** Its class boards carry a "What You Need to Play" panel; a reviewer running it concluded "A player really just needs their character board and perhaps 10 minutes of explanation and then they'll be able to jump into the game." [EN World first impressions](https://www.enworld.org/threads/dungeons-dragons-heroes-of-the-borderlands-first-impressions.715238/)
- **The DM says the formula out loud, every time, until it sticks.** "Okay, so you're choosing to attack the goblin with your crossbow, which means you roll a d20 and add your dexterity and proficiency bonuses" — repeat "consistently until automatic"; "You're the teacher here." [TheGamer DM tips](https://www.thegamer.com/dungeons-dragons-dnd-first-time-player-dm-tips/). 3 Wise DMs: let players state intent, the DM picks the roll, and "explain every outcome to reinforce learning." [3 Wise DMs](https://3wisedms.com/in-the-beginning-5-essential-tips-on-teaching-players-new-to-dd-and-ttrpgs/)
- **The recommended cheat sheet is per-character, not generic.** Ginny Di's beginner sheets (made for her parents) list, for one pregen: movement (25–30 ft, "Each square is 5 ft."), Action = "Attack, cast a spell, or use an item", the character's *single* bonus action (Healing Word / Wild Shape), and the *single* reaction ("when an enemy willingly moves out of your melee range"), plus "cantrips are castable unlimited times" vs a count of 1st-level spells. [Ginny Di](https://www.ginnydi.com/cheatsheet) · [sheet text](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1HYzLEApZDo8rGCmMKidxSzdbPIjFr0Mg-5eJAuf8hWE/mobilebasic)
- **Generic sheets compress the turn to Move + Action + Bonus + Reaction (+ free interaction).** Dump Stat's beginner sheet titles it "The Four Stages of Combat (but you only need the first two)", lists the 12–14 named actions, and warns "you are not guaranteed to be able to do" a bonus action or reaction, so "have all your bonus actions and reactions written down somewhere." [Dump Stat Adventures](https://dumpstatadventures.com/a-players-perspective/player-resources-beginners-cheatsheet) · [Kassoon crib sheet](https://www.kassoon.com/dnd/5e/crib-sheet/)
- **Index cards beat sheets for spells/abilities.** Mike Mearls: write each spell on a card — "write it down so that it'll help you remember it"; don't memorise rules, just "understand generally what your character can do." [D&D Beyond / Mearls](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/139-playing-d-d-for-the-first-time-here-are-some-tips). EN World's payn: "Get some notecards and give cheat notes for the newbs." [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/threads/not-loving-weapon-mastery-with-beginners.706981/)
- **What confuses them: derived numbers and extra decision points.** Barbarulo: ignore "proficiency bonuses for a while as well as they can be really confusing", teach one rule set per session (combat first). [D&D Beyond forum](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/general-discussion/81863-how-to-teach-new-players). Clint_L on weapon mastery: "another decision point, more tactics, more rolls — are flaws not features" for learners. [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/threads/not-loving-weapon-mastery-with-beginners.706981/)
- **Etiquette for first-timers (the recurring four):** think about your turn before it arrives; "Make listening your first priority and speaking your second priority" (Mearls); "put your phone away and pay attention", "Be Combat-Ready", "Don't be afraid to make the less optimal choice", play cooperatively (Level 1 Geek); give the character "a really fun weakness" (Mearls). [Mearls](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/139-playing-d-d-for-the-first-time-here-are-some-tips) · [Level 1 Geek](https://www.level1geek.com/blog/dnd-etiquette-rules)
- **Don't demand roleplay.** "Don't expect or demand roleplay"; accept third-person description; model it via NPCs. [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/dungeons-dragons-dnd-first-time-player-dm-tips/). Colville's opener: go round the table, "introduce your character, tell us what you're playing and what your character looks like", then a tavern server asks what they want to drink. [Colville via Sage Advice](https://www.sageadvice.eu/your-first-dungeons-dragons-session-matt-colville-2/)
- **First fights: goblins, no resistances/immunities/conditions.** "Keep fights easy and simple (at first)." [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/dungeons-dragons-dnd-first-time-player-dm-tips/). Sly Flourish's level-1 maths: fewer monsters than characters, CR ≤ 1/4, average damage ≤ 5. [Sly Flourish](https://slyflourish.com/building_1st_level_encounters.html)
- **Pace rules from a kids/first-timer DM:** "Plunge into the action", "Start the adventure at the mouth of the dungeon, not in town", fixed seating order for turns, "sixty seconds to make their case", "ninety minutes is the absolute limit", "If it's not listed, you don't have it." [Norton Creek Press](https://www.nortoncreekpress.com/wordpress/running-a-dd-game-for-first-time-players-especially-kids/)

## 2. Session zero for a group of total beginners

- **Contents (Sly Flourish):** review a one-page campaign guide; the "six truths"; how the characters connect (a shared patron "is an easy way to do it"); safety tools; character-creation guidance; house rules. Then optionally a "session 0.5": "a small challenge, some opportunity for roleplaying" before levelling to 2. [Running Session Zeros](https://slyflourish.com/running_session_zeros.html)
- **Build characters together, at the table.** Sly Flourish's checklist step 11 is "Build characters collaboratively as a group" and step 12 "Run a brief adventure if time permits." [Lazy Campaign Building Checklist](https://slyflourish.com/lazy_campaign_building_checklist.html). MakeMythic: session 0 is "one to two hours", but "if you also build characters together at the table, budget three"; for new players "building characters together at the table is worth the extra time." [MakeMythic](https://makemythic.com/blog/dnd-session-0-guide/). TheGamer: "Create characters together rather than letting newcomers navigate mechanics alone" and "Explain the classes of the game for your player to see which ones get their attention." [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/dungeons-dragons-dnd-first-time-player-dm-tips/)
- **Write it down — one page.** "The mistake that wastes a good session 0 is not writing anything down"; a shared doc with pitch, tone, boundaries, schedule, house rules, party connections — "one page is plenty." Spend "ten minutes building one or two shared connections — old comrades, a debt, a shared hometown" to avoid the tavern-strangers problem. Also agree on "phones, side conversation, food." [MakeMythic](https://makemythic.com/blog/dnd-session-0-guide/)
- **Backstory: less is more.** "I'm not nearly as interested in your backstory as I am with what you are doing now"; offer class/background suggestions that create hooks instead. [3 Wise DMs](https://3wisedms.com/in-the-beginning-5-essential-tips-on-teaching-players-new-to-dd-and-ttrpgs/)
- **Commitment: short first.** Use one-shots or "3-5 session campaigns rather than long commitments" [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/dungeons-dragons-dnd-first-time-player-dm-tips/); Angry GM: "caution the players to wait and see." [Angry GM](https://theangrygm.com/how-not-to-teach-newbies-dd/)
- **Questionnaire items DMs actually use:** experience level, tone dials (magic/action/drama), awkward themes, missing-session policy, "Phone usage (I guess it has come to this)"; Lanefan notes it is really a "Pre-Session-Zero Questionnaire". [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/threads/session-zero-questionnaire.652192/)
- **Lethality is a session-zero decision.** "If your table has agreed ahead of time to a deadly challenge, then slay away, DM!" — but "a sudden and meaningless death could leave them feeling detached from the story." [D&D Beyond](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1340-how-to-handle-character-death-in-dungeons-dragons)
- **Level 1 is the danger zone, and the fix is numeric.** "No level is more dangerous in Dungeons & Dragons than 1st level"; mitigations: +5–10 starting HP, drop instant death from massive damage, fewer monsters than PCs, CR ≤ 1/4, and "get characters to 2nd level after four hours or less of game time." [Sly Flourish](https://slyflourish.com/building_1st_level_encounters.html)
- **If a PC dies in sessions 1–3:** let the player narrate last words, "Don't be afraid of rewinding the scene" [D&D Beyond](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1340-how-to-handle-character-death-in-dungeons-dragons); hand them a hireling/NPC (Cruentus), or "take a few minutes to roll up a character" and assume the new PC "has always been part of the party from day one" (Special Unicorn); the goal is "to get the player back into the primary mode of participation with the game as quickly as possible" (Iserith). Backup characters prepared in advance is the campaign norm; running 2–4 PCs each is an OSR habit, not a beginner one. [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/threads/what-do-you-do-as-gm-if-a-pc-dies-in-the-middle-of-a-session.695655/) · [Grinning Rat](https://grinningrat.substack.com/p/so-you-died-now-what)
- **Starter-box gotcha:** HotB starting characters "lack basic equipment like rope and healing potions", so fights "end with unconscious allies waiting to revive." [bjkeeton](https://bjkeeton.substack.com/p/heroes-of-the-borderlands-starter-set-dnd-5e-review)

## 3. The DM's view of player characters

- **Step zero of prep is the characters.** "As much as we love our story, players love their characters"; "know their goddamn names"; record background, motivations, NPC connections, personal quests. Stats "won't really help you integrate the character into the game." [Sly Flourish](https://slyflourish.com/step_zero_reviewing_pcs.html)
- **The campaign sheet is mostly PCs on purpose.** "Most of the space on this sheet focuses on the PCs. This is intended to keep YOU focused on those PCs as well." Keep a running "What interesting story threads have come up based on the PCs?" — "as interesting PC-focused threads come up, jot them down." [Sly Flourish worksheet](https://slyflourish.com/5e_campaign_worksheet.html)
- **A mini DM screen per character:** player name, trained skills, passive scores, languages — "a small DM screen to keep track of trained skills, passive scores, and languages"; session notes link to character cards. [Sly Flourish / Notion](https://slyflourish.com/lazy_dnd_with_notion.html)
- **One question per character, per session.** "Shelby, what made you leave Ahoyhoy?" — write the answers into prep notes; ask for a "One Unique Thing"; reward campfire stories with inspiration. [Sly Flourish](https://slyflourish.com/learning_about_the_characters.html)
- **Per-player preference capture:** ask "How does your character feel about the direction they're headed?", a magic-item wish list by "kinds of items", and on level-up have each player describe the new option; "Be specific about it and focus it on them and their experiences." [Sly Flourish](https://slyflourish.com/getting_player_feedback.html)
- **Spotlight is tracked and prompted, not hoped for.** "Highlight questions" ("Addressing the player directly moves the spotlight to them"), "faux initiative" outside combat, per-character subplots. [Bjarke the Bard](https://www.bjarkethebard.com/blog/sharing-the-spotlight-ttrpg). A Foundry module counts spotlights per PC, sorts least-spotlighted first, resets per session. [GitHub](https://github.com/MrJamela/daggerheart-spotlight-tracker/). Recap writers "point out a highlight of each player." [Tabletop Tinker](https://tabletoptinker.substack.com/p/keeping-your-players-locked-into)
- **How tools model DM-only notes on a PC:** Kanka has five visibilities — All / Admins / Only me / Only me & Admins / Members [Kanka docs](https://docs.kanka.io/en/latest/advanced/visibility.html); "Self+Admin" on entity notes is "great for setting up backstory or relations to NPCs that the DM and player know about, but need to be kept secret from other players." [Kanka blog](https://blog.kanka.io/2020/12/16/release-0-50-map-improvements-and-entity-notes-custom-permissions/). LegendKeeper: "/secret will drop in a block that you can hide from your players." [LegendKeeper 101](https://www.legendkeeper.com/legendkeeper-101-the-basics-beyond/). Foundry's GM Notes module adds GM-only text to Actors [Foundry](https://foundryvtt.com/packages/gm-notes); the "secret block" approach leaks (players "using Ctrl+A"). [Foundry Hub](https://www.foundryvtt-hub.com/package/gm-secrets/)
- **Should the DM edit the sheet? Consensus: yes, transparently.** D&D Beyond poll: 76.3% "Unlimited Full Access"; Linklite: "If you can't trust the DM, your game is broken anyway"; Wysperra: needed for cursed items, levelling, "new player assistance". Minority: ThriKreenWarrior — discuss rather than alter; Some_Body_Else — view but not edit name/race/class. Proposed etiquette: communicate before changes, ask players to self-correct. [D&D Beyond](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/general-discussion/200579-what-access-should-dms-have-to-players-sheet). "Explaining why or how something works is better than just taking the sheet and fixing it yourself." [D&D Beyond](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/general-discussion/81568-capability-to-adjust-ability-for-dms-to-edit-pc)
- **What DMs actually edit:** ArwensDaughter — "race, class, item inventory, spells learned or prepared"; the ask is for homebrew resource uses, not stats. [D&D Beyond feedback](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/d-d-beyond-feedback/100963-dm-control-to-modify-player-character-sheets)
- **Physical boards have no notes area** — "pretty annoying in practice." [bjkeeton](https://bjkeeton.substack.com/p/heroes-of-the-borderlands-starter-set-dnd-5e-review)

## 4. First-time DM campaign lifecycle (and Heroes of the Borderlands)

- **Order of operations (Sly Flourish):** choose system/setting → informal buy-in → hook and truths → one-pager → session zero (one-pager, connections, safety, build characters, short adventure) → "Prepare three quests players can choose from" → prep next session. [Checklist](https://slyflourish.com/lazy_campaign_building_checklist.html). Table kit: "players need character sheets, pencils, and dice"; "Don't worry about your entire campaign, worry about what you'll run when the players sit down at your next session"; "Be fans of the characters." [Starting Strong](https://slyflourish.com/starting_strong_at_your_first_dnd_game.html)
- **Night-before prep is tiny:** "Know your opening scene — Write two or three sentences. That's enough."; three NPCs: "name, vibe, what they want"; day-of: dice, screen, notes, initiative tracker, "Snacks are distributed (this is not optional)". After: ask "What was your favorite moment?" and "What does your character want next?", note what players *actually* did, pick one thread. [Five Sided Die](https://www.rolldfive.com/blog/how-to-run-your-first-dnd-session)
- **What first-timers get wrong: prepping the wrong things.** "There is nothing worse than preparing too many notes for your first D&D session"; "no one fricking cares" about cheese flavours; don't railroad — "The story doesn't belong only to you." [Dungeon Goblin](https://dungeongoblin.com/blog/how-to-prepare-for-your-first-dnd-session). Forum: "Expect to make mistakes and be ok with it" (Memnosyne); say "I don't know, give me a minute to look that up" (TransmorpherDDS); re-read the section "just before the game" (ThorukDuckSlayer); drop Critical Role expectations. [D&D Beyond](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/dungeons-dragons-discussion/dungeon-masters-only/113669-first-time-dm-how-do-i-prepare)
- **Colville's session 1:** 3–4 players, pregens, start in the tavern, introductions, let the hook burst in ("they got my girl"), no map — "2 miles to the North lies the Boar forest." [Sage Advice](https://www.sageadvice.eu/your-first-dungeons-dragons-session-matt-colville-2/)
- **HotB structure:** three booklets (Keep = roleplay, Wilderness = exploration, Caves = combat), "get you playing in just 20 minutes", "30- to 90-minute game sessions", booklets split "to allow players to rotate into the DM chair", suggested order Looted Wagon → Game Trail → Bandit Ambush → Keep → Caves, opening read-aloud, rumor cards to "determine what motivates each player character", "no prep required." [D&D Beyond](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/2062-behind-the-screen-tips-for-running-heroes-of-the)
- **Actual-play numbers:** THEMNGMNT ran it with "no prep" — ~2 hours covered trail, Keep, tavern, a magic sword; combats "2-3 rounds"; "both kids wanted to keep playing." Mearls (on D&D Beyond): "Prep was maybe 10 minutes." [EN World actual play](https://www.enworld.org/threads/actual-play-heroes-of-the-borderlands-starter-set.715507/). bjkeeton: two scenarios in 4.5 h including setup, 60–90 min each, "30-50 total hours" in the box. [bjkeeton](https://bjkeeton.substack.com/p/heroes-of-the-borderlands-starter-set-dnd-5e-review)
- **Known pain points:** Play Guide sequencing "hopelessly confusing", Influence action contradiction is "a needless booby trap", milestone guidance across booklets "simply incompatible with each other", no encounter-difficulty guidance, "very limited as a stand-alone experience, but also inadequate as a pathway to learning and playing the full game." [The Alexandrian](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/52962/roleplaying-games/review-heroes-of-the-borderlands-dd-starter-set-2025). "Throw the players into the game without any story preamble"; weapon cards "require players to look at both sides" (MortarionofBarbarus). [D&D Beyond forum](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/general-discussion/229501-heroes-of-the-borderlands-brief-review-and-more). Setup "can take a while" sorting boards and cards. [Noble Knight](https://play.nobleknight.com/dungeons-dragons-starter-set-heroes-of-the-borderlands-a-first-look/)
- **WotC later patched the preamble:** new read-aloud framing and the Cult of Chaos as through-line; Clint_L: the sandbox is "by design so that new players, some very young, can run it without tracking a bunch of plot threads"; mellored: rotating DMs means "You can't have an overall plot if you swap seats." [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/threads/dungeons-dragons-provides-guidance-on-how-to-run-heroes-of-the-borderlands-as-more-traditional-campaign.715517/)
- **Advancement:** no XP; "all character advancement is handled by the accomplishment of the various scenarios", levels 1–3 only. [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/dungeons-dragons-starter-set-heroes-of-the-borderlands-review-d-ds-best-start-so-far/)

## 5. What to cut for beginners

- **What the official starter box itself cuts (the strongest evidence):** class boards omit weapon mastery; short rest is a flat "4 HP" (no Hit Dice); spell components are one line ("you can't cast spells while you're holding [a shield]"); background bonuses "pre-baked"; level 1→2 is a board flip, level 3 a swap to a subclass board; no character creation, four classes, four species, no XP. [D&D Beyond class boards](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/2045-class-boards-building-your-character-in-heroes-of) · [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/dungeons-dragons-starter-set-heroes-of-the-borderlands-review-d-ds-best-start-so-far/). Proficiency bonus is baked in "without any explanation." [Alexandrian](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/52962/roleplaying-games/review-heroes-of-the-borderlands-dd-starter-set-2025)
- **What it keeps, physically:** Heroic Inspiration as "power tokens" — handing one over "feels more momentous (and is easier to remember)"; HP as tokens ("losing tokens feels more like your character is fighting to stay standing"); spell cards with range/target icons that "make spells much easier to comprehend." [Components](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/2049-how-to-use-the-components-in-heroes-of-the) · [Class boards](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/2045-class-boards-building-your-character-in-heroes-of) · [D&D Beyond forum](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/general-discussion/229501-heroes-of-the-borderlands-brief-review-and-more)
- **Weapon mastery: defer.** "Maybe move it to level 2?" (mellored); "Everything is optional when teaching new players… start informing them more and more about what they can do" (DrJawaPhD). [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/threads/not-loving-weapon-mastery-with-beginners.706981/)
- **Proficiency bonus and skills: defer.** Ignore proficiency "for a while"; one rule set per session, "just combat in the first session rather than skills." [D&D Beyond](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/general-discussion/81863-how-to-teach-new-players)
- **Rules veterans already drop (so beginners never need):** encumbrance (Thunder Brother, BookTenTiger), spell components (DEFCON 1), cover from allies (Fanaelialae), exact movement "to never break momentum" (BookTenTiger), dim-light darkvision penalties (MarkB), even concentration checks (Raith5). [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/threads/what-rule-s-do-you-tend-to-ignore.680668/). 2024 text already makes weight optional: "You can usually carry your gear and treasure without worrying about the weight." [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/dungeons-dragons-dnd-encumbrance-explained-guide/)
- **Character options: fewer.** "Having human fighters and magic-users as the only options will simplify the all-important first sessions"; "You don't have to explain everything on the character sheet"; introduce character creation after "two or three sessions." [Norton Creek](https://www.nortoncreekpress.com/wordpress/running-a-dd-game-for-first-time-players-especially-kids/)
- **Monsters: no riders.** Avoid "multiple resistances, immunities, or conditions initially." [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/dungeons-dragons-dnd-first-time-player-dm-tips/)
- **Introduction order, synthesised from the above:** S1 = d20 + one number vs AC/DC, HP, move + action; S2 = bonus action, reactions/opportunity attacks, skills, rests (flat HP); S3 = spell slots/preparation, conditions, Heroic Inspiration; level 3 = subclass; later = weapon mastery, attunement, exhaustion, feats, encumbrance, downtime/crafting. Nothing in the sources argues for multiclassing or flanking at all for this table.

## 6. Phone-at-the-table etiquette and UX

- **Observed speed gap, phone vs paper.** A DM: "paper players were very quick" finding saves and slots while "digital players [spent] quite a lot of time…scrolling up & down"; Enrif: phones are "just too small"; HarmAssassin: the "mobile app requires too much time pecking thru menus"; Laird_Denton: "lots of scrolling." [D&D Beyond forum](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/general-discussion/211753-digital-or-paper-when-playing-in-person)
- **Where digital wins:** spell lookup is "much quicker and easier than on paper" (Linklite); "dndbeyond does everything for me" (CozmoTheGrey); "Less intimidating for new players", "click on a spell and see immediately what it does", lowers "activation energy." [D&D Beyond forum](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/general-discussion/67338-character-sheet-paper-or-digital) · [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/dungeons-dragons-paper-vs-digital-character-sheet/)
- **The distraction argument is about idle time, not the sheet.** "online computer gaming between his turns" [D&D Beyond](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/general-discussion/211753-digital-or-paper-when-playing-in-person); "the distraction machine" (sirblastalot); laptops "prevent some tabletop interaction" (AmazingAwesomator); heavy scrolling means characters need "more problem[s]" (INeedMana); "I only implement restrictions when there's actually a problem"; a "basket of fidget toys" as an alternative. [ttrpg.network](https://ttrpg.network/post/20964141)
- **Norms:** EN World poll: largest group (46.1%) "Occasionally, to look something up"; Morrus uses his phone "100% of the time. But it's all game-relevant"; tablets/laptops are judged by whether use is game-focused. [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/threads/cell-phone-and-electronic-etiquette-at-your-5e-table.478642/). Session-zero rule lists include "no cellphones" [D&D Beyond](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/general-discussion/81863-how-to-teach-new-players) and "Phone usage (I guess it has come to this)." [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/threads/session-zero-questionnaire.652192/)
- **Specific D&D Beyond mobile complaints:** shared sheets on mobile cannot update HP or spell usage [bugs](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/bugs-support/158931-shared-character-sheets-not-updating-on-mobile); app updates "sometimes taking minutes or hours" [feedback](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/d-d-beyond-feedback/d-d-beyond-mobile-app-feedback/191792-character-sheet-not-updating-in-the-app); tablet view is "a resized phone interface" [feedback](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/d-d-beyond-feedback/177402-mobile-app-on-tablet); the stats/HP header collapses on scroll [bugs](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/bugs-support/6644-header-on-character-sheet-on-mobile-site). Praise: it "lays itself out to you" showing "what dice to roll and all the modifiers" (4.7★, 90K ratings). [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/d-d-beyond-player-tools/id1501810129)

## 7. Between-session engagement

- **One-sentence prompts are the highest value per minute.** "Back in the city, how do you train as you level up?", "What letter do you write to your family back in Zobeck?"; downtime as "a handful of options – ideally ones tailored to the characters"; flash fiction 100–500 words if you have time; caveat: "Such out-of-game activities aren't for everyone." [Sly Flourish](https://slyflourish.com/roleplaying_between_sessions.html)
- **A ten-minute post-session dump, five bullets.** "A five-bullet summary beats a five-page session log. You'll actually read the bullets." Skip combat detail, shopping, rules talk. Rotate a player recap each session, paid in Inspiration — "removes DM burden, reveals what players actually remember." [Kazkar](https://kazkar.ai/blog/lazy-dm-campaign-documentation)
- **Recap at the start, 3–5 minutes, ideally by a player.** "Let players take over the recap, retelling the last session from their character's point of view"; "offering inspiration… for the person who recaps the session while in character"; outline it at session end; end sessions on a cliffhanger. [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/dungeons-dragons-dnd-engaging-interesting-session-recaps/)
- **Session reports with a per-player highlight keep chat alive.** "I try to point out a highlight of each player if I can"; "I love when the players reach out to me between sessions." [Tabletop Tinker](https://tabletoptinker.substack.com/p/keeping-your-players-locked-into)
- **Two questions at the end of every session:** "What was your favorite moment?" / "What does your character want next?" [Five Sided Die](https://www.rolldfive.com/blog/how-to-run-your-first-dnd-session); or Stars & Wishes — gentle enough that it "won't risk hurting your feelings", recommended for first-time DMs. [Troy Press](https://troypress.com/stars-wishes-feedback-for-agile-session-prep/) · [Burn After Running](https://burnafterrunningrpg.com/2022/01/07/stars-and-wishes-because-feedback-is-hard/) · [Sly Flourish](https://slyflourish.com/getting_player_feedback.html)
- **Ask, write, reuse.** "Write down the answers when you get them and add them to your game notes for your next prep session"; reward campfire stories with inspiration. [Sly Flourish](https://slyflourish.com/learning_about_the_characters.html)
- **Journaling is optional flavour, not homework.** In-character journals, shared Discord/Google Doc — "Make it easy, make it rewarding, and make it optional." [Kazkar](https://kazkar.ai/blog/build-campaign-wiki) · [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/dungeons-dragons-dnd-campaign-journal-keeping-tips-tricks/)
- **Downtime: public, resolved at the table, short enough for the recap.** [D&D Beyond forum](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/dungeons-dragons-discussion/dungeon-masters-only/59859-what-downtime-activities-do-you-offer-to-you)

## Design implications for a beginner-table companion app

1. **ADD a per-character "My turn" card pinned to the top of the phone sheet** — move, 1–2 attack lines written as "roll d20 + 5, hit if ≥ AC, then 1d8 + 3", the character's *one* bonus action, *one* reaction, cantrips vs remaining slots. This is Ginny Di's format and TheGamer's spoken formula (§1).
2. **CHANGE the sheet's default to a "Session 1" gate profile that matches the starter box's cuts**: no weapon mastery, no Hit Dice (short rest = flat HP), no components, no attunement/encumbrance, proficiency folded into final numbers. The gate system exists; its *defaults* should be HotB's (§5).
3. **ADD a gate ladder with one-screen unlock explainers** ("You just unlocked reactions: …") ordered S1 → S2 → S3 → level 3 as in §5, so the rules chapters become a curriculum rather than a library.
4. **ADD a "pick three" quick path to character creation (class, species, background) with pregens**, mirroring the 20-minute box flow; keep the 8-step wizard as the deep path (§2, §4).
5. **ADD a session-zero one-pager the DM fills in-app and players can read**: truths, party connection, lethality setting, phone rule, schedule. "One page is plenty"; "write it down" (§2).
6. **ADD level-1 safety rails as DM toggles**: +5 starting HP, no instant death from massive damage, and encounter-builder warnings when monsters outnumber PCs, CR > 1/4, or average damage > 5 (§2).
7. **ADD a death flow**: mark PC dead → prompt for last words → hand the player a hireling/NPC card for the rest of the session → new PC at party level, joining "as if always there" (§2).
8. **ADD DM-private notes per PC** (Kanka "Only me & Admins" analogue): player name, pronunciation, what the player wants, what they're nervous about, hooks, NPC ties, "one question to ask next session", open threads, spotlight tally. Never visible to other players; optionally a "shared with this player" subset (§3).
9. **CHANGE DM editing of player sheets to full-but-attributed**: allow it (76% norm, needed for cursed items, fixes, level-ups) but log every DM edit on the sheet and nudge "ask the player to fix it" first (§3).
10. **ADD a ten-minute post-session form**: five bullets, one highlight per player, one thread for next time, and the two end-of-session questions or Stars & Wishes — feeding the next prep's "step zero" (§3, §4, §7).
11. **ADD a HotB run mode**: booklet order, per-encounter "materials needed" list, opening read-aloud, rumor cards → hooks, and a single milestone ladder (already `milestone_level`) to resolve the booklets' "incompatible" milestone guidance (§4).
12. **REMOVE scroll-and-tap cost from in-play actions**: HP damage in ≤ 2 taps without leaving the sheet; attacks and slots in the first viewport; no collapsing header. Benchmark: "paper players were very quick" (§6).
13. **ADD a between-turns "phone down" mode**: a glanceable idle screen (initiative position, HP, conditions) so the phone gives nothing to scroll; the shared table screen carries initiative so players need not look at phones between turns (§6).
14. **CHANGE spells to a card metaphor with range/target icons and slot tokens**, the two things reviewers say made spells click for new players (§5, §6).
15. **ADD starting consumables to level-1 kits** (healing potion, rope) — the box's omission produced fights that ended with "unconscious allies waiting to revive" (§2).
16. **ADD one-sentence DM prompts per player between sessions**, optional, with answers landing in that PC's DM notes; and a rotating player-recap slot paid in Heroic Inspiration (§7).
17. **ADD a session-length nudge**: the box and kid-table advice converge on 60–90 minutes; surface elapsed time to the DM (§1, §4).
18. **REMOVE backstory fields from the first-session path**; replace with "one unique thing" and one hook, asked *after* session 1 (§2, §3).
19. **ADD a spotlight tally in the DM's party glance** (count per PC, least-spotlighted first, reset per session) plus a "highlight question" prompt for the quietest player (§3).
20. **KEEP dice physical and keep the sheet secondary**: everything above is aimed at making the phone something a beginner glances at, not lives in — the strongest signal across §1 and §6 is that the sheet should tell them *what to roll*, then get out of the way.
