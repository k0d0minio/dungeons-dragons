# Your turn

Most of the evening is a conversation with no order to it. Then a fight starts, or a
chase, or something else where seconds matter, and the table switches into
[[initiative|turn order]] — everyone rolls once, and from then on you go round the table in
that order until it's over.

When it's your turn, you get four things. They are not interchangeable, and mixing them up
is the single most common thing a new player gets wrong.

## The four things

**Your movement.** Up to your [[speed]] in feet — 30 for most people. You can split it:
walk a bit, do something, walk the rest. It costs you nothing to use and nothing to skip.

**One [[action]].** The main thing you do. Attack, cast a spell, shove someone over, drink
a potion, help a friend, hide, run for the door, try the lever. Almost everything
interesting is an action.

**Maybe one [[bonus-action|bonus action]].** Only if something you have specifically gives
you one — a second attack with an off-hand weapon, a rogue's dash away, a healing word.

**One [[reaction]], not on your turn.** Something that fires when a trigger happens,
usually while somebody else is acting.

Then your turn ends and it's the next person's.

## Bonus actions are not spare actions

This is the trap. A [[bonus-action|bonus action]] is **not** a second action you may spend
on whatever you like. It is a slot that only opens when a specific rule on your sheet says
"as a bonus action" — and if nothing on your sheet says that, you simply don't have one
that turn.

You cannot take a normal [[action|action]] as a bonus action. You cannot attack twice by
calling the second one a bonus action. If you're not sure whether you have one, look for
the exact words _bonus action_ on your sheet. No words, no bonus action.

The same goes the other way: a bonus action you don't use is not saved up, and it can't be
traded for movement or anything else.

## Reactions happen while you're waiting

A [[reaction]] is the one thing you do when it isn't your turn. You get one, it refreshes
at the start of your next turn, and it only fires on its trigger.

The one everybody has is the [[opportunity-attack|Opportunity Attack]]: an enemy standing
next to you walks away, and you get a free swing at them as they go. That's it — walking
_towards_ you doesn't trigger it, and neither does an enemy who carefully steps back only
as far as their own move allows.

Some spells and class features are reactions too — the shield that goes up as an arrow
arrives, the counter to somebody else's spell. They're worth remembering, because a
reaction you forget is a reaction you wasted.

> **If you remember one sentence:** move, do one thing, and keep an eye out for the moment
> to do your one out-of-turn thing.

## What "one action" actually buys you

Some useful ones, so the menu isn't a blank page when it's your turn:

- **Attack** — swing, stab, or shoot. Some classes get to attack more than once with the single Attack action later on; the sheet will say.
- **Cast a spell** — most spells take an action. See **Casting spells**.
- **Dash** — move your [[speed]] again, on top of your normal movement.
- **Disengage** — move away without giving anyone an [[opportunity-attack|Opportunity Attack]].
- **Dodge** — until your next turn, attacks against you are at [[disadvantage]]. The turn where you have no good option is rarely a wasted one.
- **Help** — give an ally [[advantage]] on their next roll, or lend a hand with a task.
- **Hide** — try to get out of sight, which usually means a roll.
- **Ready** — pick a trigger now and a thing to do when it happens, and spend your [[reaction]] on it later.
- **Search**, **Influence**, **Study**, **Utilize** — look for something, talk somebody round, recall what you know, or use an object. All actions, all fair game.

## The order of a turn is up to you

There is no fixed sequence. Step forward, swing, step back. Or drink the potion, then
move. Or move, cast, and use the rest of your movement to get behind a pillar. Do them in
whatever order helps.

The one thing you can't do is take your action in the middle of a _single_ movement of one
square — but you'll never notice that, because you can just move some, act, and move the
rest.

## When it isn't your turn

Watch. Two reasons. First, your [[reaction]] might come up. Second, the situation you plan
for on somebody else's turn is not the situation you'll be in on yours — the door will be
open, the ally will be down, the goblin will have moved.

Deciding what you'll do _while other people take their turns_ is the difference between a
fight that takes twenty minutes and one that takes an hour.
