# Rolling the d20

One die does almost all the work: the twenty-sided one, the big round-ish one. When
something in the game is genuinely uncertain, you pick it up, roll it, and add a number
from your sheet. The rules call the whole move a [[d20-test|D20 Test]].

This app never rolls for you. That's on purpose — the dice on the table are half the point
of sitting at one. What these pages do is tell you which die to pick up and what to add.

## The shape of every roll

Roll the d20. Add the right [[modifier]]. Add your
[[proficiency-bonus|proficiency bonus]] if you're proficient. Compare the total to a
number.

That's it. Three kinds of roll use that shape, and telling them apart is most of what you
need.

## The three kinds

**An [[ability-check|ability check]]** — _can I do this thing?_ Climbing the wall,
picking the lock, spotting the tripwire, haggling the merchant down. You are being asked
whether you manage it. Compare your total to a
[[difficulty-class|Difficulty Class]] — a number Jamie has in mind for how hard it is. Meet
it or beat it and you succeed.

**An [[attack-roll|attack roll]]** — _do I hit?_ Compare your total to the target's
[[armour-class|Armour Class]]. Equal or higher, you hit, and then you roll damage with a
different die.

**A [[saving-throw|saving throw]]** — _does this thing get me?_ The dragon breathes fire,
the floor gives way, the spell tries to charm you. Something is happening _to_ you and
you're trying to resist it. Compare your total to whatever DC the danger has.

The quick tell: a check is you reaching out, a save is the world reaching in, an attack is
you trying to land a hit.

## Which modifier do I add?

The one that fits, and you almost never have to guess — the sheet already pairs them up.

- **Ability check** — whichever of the six abilities the task leans on. Climbing is Strength, sneaking is Dexterity, remembering the legend is Intelligence. If it's a named [[skill]] like Stealth or Persuasion, your sheet lists the total to use.
- **Attack roll** — Strength for most melee weapons, Dexterity for bows, thrown things and anything _finesse_ (where you may pick either). Spells use your [[spellcasting-ability|spellcasting ability]] instead.
- **Saving throw** — the ability named by whatever is attacking you. A net wants Dexterity, poison wants Constitution, a spell that gets inside your head wants Wisdom or Charisma. Jamie will say which.

## When proficiency applies

Your [[proficiency-bonus|proficiency bonus]] is one number that grows with your level —
+2 at the start. You add it when you're **trained in this particular thing**, and not
otherwise.

You add it to:

- checks using a [[skill]] you're proficient in, and to tools you're trained with
- attacks with weapons you're proficient in, and to every spell attack you make
- [[saving-throw|saving throws]] in the two abilities your [[class]] made you proficient in — those two are marked on your sheet and they never change

You do **not** add it to a check with a skill you don't have, or to a save in an ability
your class didn't cover. You still roll — untrained isn't hopeless, it's just the raw
ability with no training on top.

A few characters have [[expertise]] in a skill or two, which doubles the proficiency bonus
for that skill only. If you have it, your sheet has already done the doubling.

## Natural 20s and natural 1s

On an [[attack-roll|attack roll]], a natural 20 — the die itself showing 20, before you add
anything — always hits, and it's a [[critical-hit|critical hit]]: roll the damage dice
twice and add both. A natural 1 always misses, however good your modifier is.

On checks and saves, a 20 or a 1 is just a 20 or a 1. High and low, but no special rule.

## Advantage and disadvantage

Sometimes the situation is tilted. You have the high ground, or you're trying to pick a
lock in the dark.

[[advantage|Advantage]] means roll two d20s and use the higher. [[disadvantage|Disadvantage]]
means roll two and use the lower. You still add your modifiers once, to the die you kept.

Two things worth knowing. They don't stack — three reasons for advantage is still just two
dice. And they cancel: one of each and you roll one die as normal, no matter how many
reasons there were on either side.

Practically, it's worth about five points, and it's the biggest swing in the game you can
actually arrange. Asking "is there any way I'd have advantage on this?" is almost always a
better use of your turn than a clever plan.

## Which die for damage

The d20 decides _whether_. A different die decides _how much_. Your weapon or spell names
it — a dagger does d4, a longsword d8, a greataxe d12, a fireball a fistful of d6s. Roll
that die (or those dice), add the same ability modifier you used to attack if it's a
weapon, and tell Jamie the total.

Damage rolls never take your [[proficiency-bonus|proficiency bonus]]. Proficiency helps you
hit, not hurt.

## Rolls you never make

Two useful non-rolls.

If a task has no real chance of failure and no pressure on you, there's no roll — you
just do it. Asking to roll for something you could obviously manage only introduces a way
to fail.

And your [[passive-perception|Passive Perception]] is a standing number, on your sheet,
that Jamie compares against things you might notice without looking. You never roll it. It
is why you sometimes get told about the tripwire before anyone says anything.
