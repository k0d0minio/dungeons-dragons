# Casting spells

Skip this page if your character doesn't cast — a fighter, a barbarian and most rogues can
live a whole campaign without it. If yours does cast, this is the bookkeeping that catches
people out, and it's four ideas rather than forty.

## Cantrips are free, forever

A [[cantrip]] is a small spell you know so well it costs you nothing. Cast it every turn of
every fight if you like; it never runs out and nothing needs ticking off. Most casters have
one that does damage, and it is the honest answer to "what do I do this turn" more often
than new players expect.

Cantrips get stronger on their own as your character levels up. You don't choose to
upgrade them — the numbers just go up.

## Everything else costs a slot

Every other spell burns a [[spell-slot|spell slot]]. Slots are the fuel: you have a fixed
number of them per level of spell, they're printed on your sheet, and they come back on a
[[long-rest|long rest]].

Two things about slots.

**The spell's level and the slot's level are different things.** A level 1 spell needs a
slot of level 1 _or higher_. Spending a bigger slot on a smaller spell often makes it
stronger — that's [[higher-level-spell|casting at a higher level]], and the spell's own
text says what improves. Spending a bigger slot is legal even when nothing improves; it's
just wasteful.

**Slots are not per spell.** They're a shared pool. Casting the same spell four times and
casting four different spells cost exactly the same.

## Known, prepared, and the difference

This is the one that tangles people.

Some casters have a fixed list of spells they know and that's that. Others — clerics,
druids, wizards, paladins — have a bigger pool and choose which ones are _ready today_.
Those chosen ones are your [[prepared-spell|prepared spells]], and they're the only ones
you can cast until you change them.

Changing what's prepared happens after a [[long-rest|long rest]], not mid-fight. So the
question "which spells do I prepare?" is a question you answer at the start of a day of
adventuring, and then live with.

Preparing a spell doesn't cost a slot. Casting it does. Having a spell prepared and having
a slot left are two separate checks, and you need both.

## Concentration: one at a time

Some spells keep going after you cast them — the light that follows you, the ally who
keeps hitting harder, the enemy tangled in vines. Those need
[[concentration|concentration]], and you can only concentrate on **one** at a time.

Cast a second concentration spell and the first one ends, immediately, whatever you were
hoping. This is the rule that quietly decides whether a caster is any good in a fight: your
turn is rarely "which spell", it's "is this better than the one I'm already holding".

Concentration also breaks if you take damage and fail a [[saving-throw|saving throw]] to
hold on to it — Constitution, DC 10 or half the damage you took, whichever is higher. So a
caster who wants to keep a spell up should think hard about where they're standing.

It ends too if you're knocked out, and you can drop it deliberately at any time for free.

## The rest of the bookkeeping

**Your [[spellcasting-ability|spellcasting ability]]** is one of Intelligence, Wisdom or
Charisma, decided by your class. It feeds two numbers on your sheet: the
[[spell-attack-roll|spell attack]] bonus you add when a spell makes you roll to hit, and
your [[spell-save-dc|spell save DC]] — the number an enemy has to beat when your spell
attacks _them_.

Every spell does one or the other, never both. Read the spell: either you roll against
their [[armour-class|Armour Class]], or they roll against your save DC.

**[[spell-components|Components]]** are what casting looks like from outside — words, a
gesture, sometimes a physical ingredient. It matters in two situations: you can't cast a
spell with a spoken part while gagged or silenced, and you need a hand free for the
gestures. Ordinary ingredients are covered by a spellcasting focus or a component pouch and
nobody tracks them.

**[[ritual|Rituals]]** are the free lunch. A handful of spells carry the ritual tag; if
yours does, and your class allows it, you can cast it by spending ten extra minutes instead
of a slot. Out of combat, with time to spare, always ritual it.

**[[area-of-effect|Areas of effect]]** are the cone, the sphere, the line. They don't care
whose side anyone is on. Check where your friends are standing before the fireball, not
after.

## The one-slot-per-turn rule

You may cast whatever your action allows. But if you use a
[[bonus-action|bonus action]] to cast a spell that costs a slot, then the only other spell
you can cast that turn is a [[cantrip]] — no second slot on the same turn.

It's easy to forget because it only bites when you have a bonus-action spell in the first
place. If you do, it's the rule that shapes your turns.

## What this looks like in play

A good caster's turn is usually one of three things. Keep the concentration spell you've
already got running and throw a [[cantrip]]. Or decide the fight has changed and spend a
slot on something better. Or do the unglamorous thing — help, move, get line of sight —
because none of the spells are worth it yet.

Burning your best slot on round one of every fight is the mistake. There is no prize for
finishing the day with slots unspent, but there's no prize for finishing round two with
none either.
