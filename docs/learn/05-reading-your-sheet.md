# Reading your sheet

Your character sheet looks like a tax form the first time you open it. It isn't: it's about
eight numbers that matter and a lot of numbers that exist only to produce those eight.

This page walks the ones you'll be asked for out loud, and says where each of them came
from — because a number you understand is a number you'll remember.

## The six that everything else comes from

Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma. Each is an
[[ability-score|ability score]], usually somewhere between 8 and 17 when you start.

The score itself is almost never used. What gets used is its [[modifier]] — the small
number beside it, from about −1 to +3 at first level. That's what you add to rolls. The
sheet works it out for you: every two points of score above 10 is another +1, and below 10
it goes the other way.

If you take one thing from this page: **the little number is the one you add.**

## Armour Class

Your [[armour-class|Armour Class]] is how hard you are to hit. An enemy's
[[attack-roll|attack roll]] has to reach it or beat it.

It starts at 10 and goes up from what you're wearing and carrying. Light armour adds its
own number plus your Dexterity modifier; heavy armour is a flat number and ignores
Dexterity entirely; a shield is +2 on top of any of it. With no armour at all you're on 10
plus Dexterity, which is why a nimble character in normal clothes isn't hopeless.

The sheet adds this up from your inventory, so equipping the armour is what changes the
number — there's nothing to type in.

Typical is 13 to 18. Under 12 and you'll get hit a lot; that's a real choice some
characters make on purpose.

## Initiative

At the top of a fight everyone rolls [[initiative]] once: a d20 plus your Dexterity
modifier. Highest goes first, and that order holds for the whole fight.

So your initiative bonus _is_ your Dexterity modifier, with nothing else in it for most
characters. It's on the sheet so you don't have to go looking for it while five people
wait.

You roll this one on the physical dice like everything else — the app tracks the order once
the numbers are called out.

## Hit points

Your [[hit-points|hit points]] are how much punishment you can absorb. They go down when
you're hurt and back up when you're healed or you take a [[long-rest|long rest]].

At zero you're not dead — you're unconscious and rolling
[[death-saving-throw|death saving throws]] each turn, and a friend reaching you is usually
enough. This is the game being deliberately forgiving. Characters die, but almost never
without someone getting a chance to stop it.

Your maximum came from your [[class|class's]] hit die plus your Constitution modifier, and
it grows every level.

Two extras that sit next to it. [[temporary-hit-points|Temporary hit points]] are a
separate buffer that soaks damage first and doesn't stack with another lot — you keep the
better one. [[hit-dice|Hit dice]] are the pool you spend on a
[[short-rest|short rest]] to heal up a bit without burning the whole day.

## Saving throws

Six of them, one per ability, and two are marked as proficient — your
[[class]] chose which two when you made the character, and they never change. Those two
get your [[proficiency-bonus|proficiency bonus]] added; the other four are the bare
[[modifier]].

The marked pair is worth knowing by heart. It's the difference between shrugging off the
dragon and not.

## Skills

Eighteen [[skill|skills]], each tied to one ability. Being proficient in a skill means
adding your [[proficiency-bonus|proficiency bonus]] to checks with it, and your
[[background]] and class picked those for you.

The sheet shows the finished total, so when Jamie says "give me a Stealth check", you read
one number off the screen and add it to the die. You don't build it up from parts at the
table.

A couple of characters have [[expertise]] in a skill or two — double proficiency, already
included in the total shown.

## Passive Perception

One standing number, never rolled: 10 plus your Perception total. Jamie compares it
quietly against things you might notice while just walking around. Nothing for you to do —
but it's why noticing things is a real reason to care about Wisdom.

## Spell save DC

Only if you cast. Your [[spell-save-dc|spell save DC]] is the number an enemy must beat when
your spell forces them to resist it, and it's 8 plus your
[[proficiency-bonus|proficiency bonus]] plus your
[[spellcasting-ability|spellcasting ability]] modifier. Around 13 to 15 at low levels.

Your [[spell-attack-roll|spell attack]] bonus is the same thing without the 8 — the number
you add when the spell makes you roll to hit instead.

## Attacks

Each attack line carries two numbers: what you add to the d20 to hit, and what you roll for
damage. The to-hit number is your ability [[modifier]] plus your
[[proficiency-bonus|proficiency bonus]]; the damage is the weapon's die plus the same
ability modifier, with no proficiency in it.

The sheet has already assembled both. At the table you read the line, roll, and say the
total.

## The rest of the screen

The other cards on your sheet are things that change during a session rather than things
you look up: your [[spell-slot|slots]], your class's own limited resources, any
[[condition|conditions]] you've picked up, [[exhaustion]], your money and gear, your notes.
They're there to be tapped mid-fight so you don't lose track — not to be memorised.

## The honest summary

Before session one, know roughly where these five live and you're fine:

- your [[armour-class|AC]]
- your current and maximum [[hit-points|hit points]]
- your [[initiative]] bonus
- the to-hit and damage numbers on your main attack
- your two proficient [[saving-throw|saving throws]]

Everything else you can find while somebody else takes their turn.
