# 05 — Combat

> Purpose: the complete 2024 combat loop (SRD 5.2.1) stated exactly — ordered sequences, state machines and numeric tables — for DMs running sessions and players at the table.

## The combat sequence

Combat proceeds in this order:

1. **Establish positions.** The DM says where everyone is.
2. **Roll initiative.** Everyone rolls once; the order holds for the whole fight.
3. **Take turns** in initiative order. One turn each is a **round**.
4. **Repeat** until one side is dead, fled or done fighting.

### Surprise

There is no surprise round and no "you lose your first turn". A creature that is caught unawares — the DM's call, usually because it failed to notice a hidden enemy — has **Disadvantage on its initiative roll**. That is the whole rule.

Two conditions also touch initiative: a creature that is **Incapacitated** when it rolls has Disadvantage, and a creature that is **Invisible** has Advantage.

> **Changed from 2014:** surprise used to cost a creature its entire first turn and its reactions. It now costs it a place in the order. Ambushes still work — they just do not remove a player from the game for a round.

### Initiative

- Everyone rolls a **Dexterity check**: d20 + Dexterity modifier. No Proficiency Bonus unless something grants it, and the Alert feat is what usually does.
- The DM may roll one initiative for a group of identical monsters.
- Order is highest first, and it does not change between rounds.
- Ties are the DM's to settle. Pick a convention at session zero and stick to it: higher Dexterity score wins, or the tied creatures roll off, or players win ties against monsters.

### Rounds and turns

| Unit | Duration | Contains |
|---|---|---|
| **Round** | 6 seconds of in-world time | One turn for every participant, in initiative order |
| **Turn** | Your slice of the round | Movement, one action, possibly a Bonus Action, and free interactions |
| **Minute** | 10 rounds | — |

Something that lasts "until the end of your next turn" runs into the following round. Something that happens "at the start of your turn" resolves before you move or act.

## Your turn

On your turn you can:

- **Move** up to your Speed, split up however you like.
- Take **one action**.
- Take **one Bonus Action**, but only if a feature, spell or item grants one. There is no default Bonus Action.
- Interact with **one object** for free — draw a weapon, open a door, pull a lever. A second interaction is the **Utilize** action.
- Speak briefly.
- Take a **Reaction** when something triggers it, on anyone's turn including your own.

You can do any, all or none of these.

### The twelve actions

The 2024 rules collapsed the old list into twelve named actions. Learn these and you have the whole turn.

| Action | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Attack** | Make one attack roll with a weapon or an Unarmed Strike. **Extra Attack** makes more attacks part of this one action, and you may move between them |
| **Dash** | Give yourself extra movement equal to your Speed for the rest of the turn |
| **Disengage** | Your movement does not provoke Opportunity Attacks for the rest of the turn |
| **Dodge** | Until the start of your next turn, attack rolls against you have Disadvantage and you make Dexterity saving throws with Advantage. Lost if you become Incapacitated or your Speed drops to 0 |
| **Help** | Assist an ally's ability check (you must be proficient in the skill or tool) or their attack roll against a target within 5 feet of you, giving them Advantage — or administer first aid to stabilise a dying creature |
| **Hide** | Make a **DC 15** Dexterity (Stealth) check. On a success you gain the **Invisible** condition |
| **Influence** | Make a Charisma (Deception, Intimidation, Performance or Persuasion) or Wisdom (Animal Handling) check to change a creature's attitude |
| **Magic** | Cast a spell, use a magic item, or use a magical feature |
| **Ready** | Name a perceivable trigger and a response. When the trigger fires you take the response as your **Reaction** |
| **Search** | Make a Wisdom (Insight, Medicine, Perception or Survival) check |
| **Study** | Make an Intelligence (Arcana, History, Investigation, Nature or Religion) check |
| **Utilize** | Use a nonmagical object |

Anything not on the list is improvised and adjudicated by the DM, usually with an ability check.

> **Changed from 2014:** Cast a Spell, Use an Object and Use a Magic Item became **Magic** and **Utilize**; Search split into **Search** (senses) and **Study** (knowledge); **Influence** gave the social roll an action of its own; and Hide became a flat DC rather than a contest.

### Hiding, in detail

The Hide action needs three things: you must be **Heavily Obscured** or behind at least **three-quarters cover**, you must be out of any enemy's line of sight, and you must beat **DC 15** on a Dexterity (Stealth) check.

On a success you have the **Invisible** condition — attacks against you have Disadvantage, yours have Advantage, and you are not affected by anything that needs to see you. **Write down the Stealth total**: a creature that takes the Search action rolls Wisdom (Perception) against it, and finds you if it equals or beats it.

The condition ends the moment you make a sound louder than a whisper, an enemy finds you, you make an attack roll, or you cast a spell with a verbal component.

### Readying

- Only an action can be readied, and only against a trigger you can perceive.
- Readying a **spell** casts it now: the slot is spent and the components are used, and you hold the energy with **Concentration**. If Concentration breaks first, the spell and the slot are gone.
- A readied action you never release simply expires at the start of your next turn.
- Releasing it uses your Reaction, so you cannot also make an Opportunity Attack that round.

### Bonus Actions

- At most **one** per turn, and only when something grants it by name.
- You choose when in your turn to take it, unless the feature says otherwise.
- Anything that takes away your actions — the **Incapacitated** condition — takes your Bonus Action too.

### Reactions

- **One per round**, refreshing at the start of *your* turn, not at the start of the round.
- A Reaction can be taken on any creature's turn. If it interrupts someone, they finish their turn once it resolves.
- The Incapacitated condition prevents Reactions.

### Opportunity Attacks

**Provokes**: a creature **you can see** leaves your reach using its movement, its action or its Reaction.

**Does not provoke**:

- Teleporting.
- Being moved without spending its own movement — shoved, pulled, thrown, or carried.
- Moving *within* your reach. Circling you at 5 feet never provokes; only leaving does.
- A creature that took the **Disengage** action this turn.

The attack is **one melee attack**, taken as a Reaction, resolved just before the creature leaves your reach.

## Movement

Your budget each turn is your Speed. Split it freely around and between actions and attacks.

| Situation | Cost |
|---|---|
| Normal | 1 ft per foot |
| **Difficult terrain** | 1 extra foot per foot |
| **Climbing, swimming or crawling** without a matching Speed | 1 extra foot per foot |
| Climbing or swimming through difficult terrain | 2 extra feet per foot |
| **Squeezing** into a space a size smaller than you | 1 extra foot per foot |
| **Standing up from Prone** | Half your Speed, rounded down, as a flat cost |
| **Dropping Prone** | Free |

- Difficult terrain does not stack with itself. An area is difficult or it is not.
- **Squeezing** also gives you Disadvantage on attack rolls and Dexterity saves, and gives attackers Advantage against you.
- **Moving through a creature's space**: freely through an ally's; through an enemy's only if it is at least two sizes larger or smaller than you. Either way the space counts as difficult terrain, and you cannot end your move there.
- If you have several Speeds — walk, fly, swim, climb — moving with one subtracts from all of them.
- A flying creature **falls** if it is knocked Prone, its Speed drops to 0, or it otherwise cannot move, unless it can hover.
- Being moved on someone else's turn does not cost you movement.

## Making an attack

Every attack follows three steps: choose a target in range, work out the modifiers, then roll and resolve.

```
attack roll = d20 + ability modifier + Proficiency Bonus (if proficient) + other bonuses
hit if the total is at least the target's AC
```

- **Melee weapon**: Strength, or Dexterity with a **Finesse** weapon.
- **Ranged weapon**: Dexterity, or the melee ability for a **Thrown** melee weapon like a Javelin.
- **Spell attack**: your spellcasting ability plus your Proficiency Bonus — you are always proficient with your own spells.
- **Unarmed Strike**: Strength plus your Proficiency Bonus.
- **Natural 20** hits and crits. **Natural 1** misses. Under the 2024 rules those also apply to checks and saves, but on an attack the natural 20 is what makes a Critical Hit.
- **Reach** is 5 feet unless the weapon or creature says otherwise; a Reach weapon adds 5 feet.
- **Range (X/Y)**: beyond X and up to Y is Disadvantage; beyond Y is impossible.

### Unarmed Strike — three options

Every creature has one, and the 2024 rules make it a real choice. Take the Attack action, and instead of a weapon attack, make an Unarmed Strike and pick one:

| Option | Resolution |
|---|---|
| **Damage** | An attack roll. On a hit, `1 + your Strength modifier` bludgeoning damage |
| **Grapple** | No attack roll. The target makes a **Strength or Dexterity saving throw** (its choice) against `8 + your Strength modifier + your Proficiency Bonus`. On a failure it has the **Grappled** condition. The target must be no more than one size larger than you, and you need a free hand |
| **Shove** | The same saving throw. On a failure, push it 5 feet away or give it the **Prone** condition — your choice |

> **Changed from 2014:** grappling and shoving are no longer contests. The attacker's number is static, the defender rolls, and both are options of a single Unarmed Strike rather than special attacks with their own rules.

**Escaping a Grapple**: the Grappled creature takes an action to make a Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check against the same DC. The Grapple also ends if the grappler is Incapacitated or if something moves the target out of the grappler's reach.

**Moving a grappled creature**: the grappler can drag or carry it, but every foot of movement costs an extra foot unless the target is Tiny or at least two sizes smaller.

### Weapon Mastery

Barbarians, fighters, paladins, rangers and rogues can use the **mastery property** printed on each weapon. It applies automatically on a hit — there is nothing to spend and nothing to declare in advance.

| Property | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Cleave** | After a melee hit, make one more melee attack roll against a second creature within 5 feet of the first and in your reach. No ability modifier on that damage. Once per turn |
| **Graze** | On a **miss**, the target still takes damage equal to the ability modifier you attacked with, of the weapon's type |
| **Nick** | The Light property's extra attack becomes part of the Attack action instead of a Bonus Action. Once per turn |
| **Push** | On a hit, push a Large or smaller target up to 10 feet straight away |
| **Sap** | On a hit, the target has Disadvantage on its next attack roll before the start of your next turn |
| **Slow** | On a hit that deals damage, reduce the target's Speed by 10 feet until the start of your next turn. Never more than 10 feet total |
| **Topple** | On a hit, the target makes a Constitution save against `8 + your attack ability modifier + your Proficiency Bonus` or is knocked **Prone** |
| **Vex** | On a hit that deals damage, you have Advantage on your next attack roll against that creature before the end of your next turn |

Which weapon carries which property is in `08-equipment.md`. How many kinds of weapon a class can use masteries with is in `04-classes.md`.

### Two-weapon fighting

The 2024 rules moved this onto the **Light** property. When you take the Attack action and attack with a Light weapon, you may make one extra attack as a **Bonus Action** with a *different* Light weapon. You do not add your ability modifier to the extra attack's damage unless the modifier is negative, or you have the Two-Weapon Fighting style.

A weapon with the **Nick** mastery — Dagger, Light Hammer, Scimitar, Sickle — lets you make that extra attack as part of the Attack action instead, freeing the Bonus Action for something else.

### Ranged attacks in melee

Making a ranged attack while a hostile creature that can see you and is not Incapacitated is within 5 feet of you gives you **Disadvantage**.

### Casting in combat

- A spell with a casting time of an action is the **Magic** action.
- **You can cast only one spell that expends a spell slot on a turn.** A second slot-spending spell is not available even with Action Surge, and this is what stops a paladin smiting twice or a wizard chaining two leveled spells.
- Cantrips are unaffected by that limit; you may cast as many as your actions allow.

> **Changed from 2014:** the old restriction was specifically about bonus-action spells. The 2024 rule is broader and simpler — one slot per turn, whatever action you spend.

## Advantage and disadvantage in combat

Advantage is 2d20 keep high, Disadvantage is 2d20 keep low, they never stack, and any of one plus any of the other cancels to a single d20.

| You attack with **Advantage** when… | You attack with **Disadvantage** when… |
|---|---|
| You have the Invisible condition and the target cannot see you | You cannot see the target |
| The target is **Prone** and you are within 5 feet | The target is **Prone** and you are more than 5 feet away |
| The target is **Restrained**, **Stunned**, **Paralyzed** or **Unconscious** | You are **Prone**, **Restrained**, **Poisoned** or **Frightened** of a visible source |
| An ally Helped you against that target | You make a ranged attack with a hostile creature within 5 feet |
| Your last hit on it had the **Vex** mastery | The target took the **Dodge** action |
| The target is Paralyzed or Unconscious and you are within 5 feet — that hit is also an **automatic Critical Hit** | You are at **long range**, or **squeezing**, or the target's last hit on you had **Sap** |

## Conditions in combat

The full text is in `07-conditions.md`. A condition never stacks with itself: two sources give one instance, each ending on its own terms.

| Condition | Combat effect |
|---|---|
| **Blinded** | Auto-fails sight-based checks; attacks against it have Advantage, its attacks have Disadvantage |
| **Charmed** | Cannot attack or harmfully target the charmer; the charmer has Advantage on social checks with it |
| **Frightened** | Disadvantage on checks and attacks while the source is in line of sight; cannot willingly move closer |
| **Grappled** | Speed 0 and cannot be increased; Disadvantage attacking anyone but the grappler; the grappler can drag it |
| **Incapacitated** | No action, Bonus Action or Reaction; Concentration broken; cannot speak; Disadvantage on initiative |
| **Invisible** | Advantage on initiative; concealed; attacks against it have Disadvantage, its attacks have Advantage |
| **Paralyzed** | Incapacitated, Speed 0, auto-fails STR and DEX saves; attacks against it have Advantage; hits from within 5 feet are Critical Hits |
| **Petrified** | Incapacitated, Speed 0, Resistance to all damage, Immunity to Poisoned, auto-fails STR and DEX saves |
| **Poisoned** | Disadvantage on attack rolls and ability checks |
| **Prone** | Crawl only, or spend half Speed to stand; Disadvantage on its attacks; attacks against it have Advantage within 5 feet and Disadvantage beyond |
| **Restrained** | Speed 0; attacks against it have Advantage, its attacks have Disadvantage; Disadvantage on DEX saves |
| **Stunned** | Incapacitated, Speed 0, auto-fails STR and DEX saves; attacks against it have Advantage |
| **Unconscious** | Incapacitated and Prone, drops what it holds, unaware; auto-fails STR and DEX saves; attacks against it have Advantage; hits from within 5 feet are Critical Hits |
| **Exhaustion** | Every level is −2 on every D20 Test and −5 feet of Speed. Six levels is death. A Long Rest removes one level |

## Cover

Only the most protective degree applies, and only against attacks coming from the far side of the cover.

| Cover | Grants | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Half cover** | +2 AC and +2 Dexterity saves | A low wall, furniture, another creature |
| **Three-quarters cover** | +5 AC and +5 Dexterity saves | A portcullis, an arrow slit, a thick trunk |
| **Total cover** | Cannot be targeted directly by an attack or a spell | Fully behind a wall |

## Damage and healing

### Damage rolls

```
damage = the weapon's or spell's dice + the ability modifier (for a weapon; for a spell only if it says so) + bonuses
```

An effect that hits several targets at once is rolled **once** and applied to all of them.

### Critical hits

Roll **all** of the attack's damage dice twice — weapon dice, Sneak Attack, a Giant Ancestry rider, everything — then add the modifiers **once**. Flat bonuses are never doubled. A greataxe crit with +3 Strength is `2d12 + 3`, not `2 × (1d12 + 3)`.

### Damage types

Thirteen: acid, bludgeoning, cold, fire, force, lightning, necrotic, piercing, poison, psychic, radiant, slashing, thunder. The type carries no rules of its own — it exists so Resistance, Vulnerability and Immunity have something to key off.

### Resistance and vulnerability

1. Total the damage of that type: dice, modifiers, flat bonuses and reductions.
2. Apply **Resistance**: halve it, rounding down.
3. Apply **Vulnerability**: double it.
4. Two sources of Resistance to the same instance count once. Same for Vulnerability.
5. **Immunity** short-circuits everything to 0.

Resistance goes last, after every other change to the number. Twenty-five bludgeoning into a creature with bludgeoning Resistance and a −5 reduction is `25 − 5 = 20`, halved to **10**.

### Dropping to 0 hit points

- Hit points run from 0 to your maximum. Damage past 0 is discarded, except for the check below.
- **Instant death**: if the damage reduces you to 0 *and* the leftover is at least your hit point maximum, you die outright. Six hit points of 30, hit for 38: 32 left over, which is at least 30, so dead.
- Otherwise you gain the **Unconscious** condition and start **dying**.
- A monster at 0 simply dies, unless the DM decides otherwise for a named NPC.

### Death saving throws

While dying, at the **start of each of your turns**, roll a plain d20 with no modifiers against **DC 10**.

| Event | Result |
|---|---|
| Roll 10–19 | One success |
| Roll 2–9 | One failure |
| **Natural 1** | Two failures |
| **Natural 20** | You regain 1 hit point immediately; the counters reset |
| Three successes | **Stable**; the counters reset |
| Three failures | **Dead** |
| Any damage while at 0 | One failure — two if it was a Critical Hit; instant death if the damage is at least your maximum |
| Any healing | Conscious at that total, dying ends, and both counters reset |
| Stabilised by someone else | The **Help** action and a DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine) check, or a spell such as Spare the Dying |

**Stable** means still at 0 and Unconscious but no longer rolling. Taking damage while stable starts the dying again. A stable creature nobody heals regains 1 hit point after 1d4 hours.

Successes and failures never carry over between episodes: both reset whenever you regain hit points or become stable.

### Healing

- Healing tops out at your maximum; the excess is lost.
- Any healing at 0 hit points restores consciousness. You are still Prone — standing costs half your Speed.
- **Healing does nothing for the dead.** Three failures or massive damage needs a spell that raises the dead, not one that closes wounds.

### Temporary hit points

- A separate pool. Damage eats temporary hit points first, then real ones.
- **They do not stack.** Given a second lot, keep the higher number; you never add them.
- They are not healing. Given to a creature at 0 hit points they neither restore consciousness nor stabilise it.
- Damage soaked by them is still damage taken, so a dying creature whose temporary hit points absorb a hit still takes the death save failure.
- They last until spent or until you finish a Long Rest.

### Knocking a creature out

When a melee attack would reduce a creature to 0 hit points, you may instead leave it on 1 hit point with the **Unconscious** condition. It stays out for an hour or until it regains any hit points. The choice is made at the moment the damage lands, costs nothing, and changes no roll.

## Mounted combat

- **Mounting or dismounting** a willing creature one size larger with the right anatomy costs movement equal to half your Speed.
- If the mount is moved against its will or knocked Prone while you ride, make a **DC 10 Dexterity saving throw** or fall off Prone within 5 feet. The same applies if you are knocked Prone.
- A **controlled** mount acts on your initiative, moves as you direct, and may take only the Dash, Disengage or Dodge action.
- An **independent** mount keeps its own initiative and acts as it likes.
- Attackers choose whether to target you or the mount.

## Underwater combat

- **Melee attacks** have Disadvantage unless the attacker has a Swim Speed or uses a Dagger, Javelin, Shortsword, Spear or Trident.
- **Ranged attacks** miss automatically beyond normal range, and have Disadvantage within it, unless the weapon is a crossbow, a net, or a thrown weapon such as a Javelin, Spear, Trident or Dart.
- Creatures and objects fully immersed have **Resistance to fire damage**.

## Improvised damage

For a hazard with no stat block, pick by how bad it is.

| Dice | Severity | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1d10 | Minor | Burned by coals, hit by a falling shelf |
| 2d10 | Dangerous | Struck by a swinging beam, splashed with acid |
| 4d10 | Serious | Caught in falling rubble, grazed by a lightning strike |
| 10d10 | Deadly | Crushed between closing walls, dropped into a vat of acid |
| 18d10 | Almost certainly fatal | Submerged in lava, under a colossal falling object |
| 24d10 | Cataclysmic | The heart of an elemental vortex |

**Falling**: 1d6 bludgeoning per 10 feet, to a maximum of 20d6, and you land with the **Prone** condition unless you avoid the damage entirely. A falling creature descends 500 feet in a round.

**Suffocation**: you can hold your breath for `1 + your Constitution modifier` minutes, minimum 30 seconds. When it runs out you drop to 0 hit points and are dying, and no healing or stabilising works until you can breathe.

## Common table rulings

**Q: Can I move, attack, then keep moving?**
A: Yes. Movement splits freely around actions and between the attacks of Extra Attack. Only the total is capped.

**Q: Does circling an enemy provoke an Opportunity Attack?**
A: No. Only *leaving* its reach does. Teleporting and forced movement never provoke.

**Q: Do Critical Hits double Sneak Attack dice?**
A: Yes. A crit doubles every die the attack deals, from any source. It never doubles a flat modifier.

**Q: Is a natural 20 an automatic success on a saving throw?**
A: Yes, under the 2024 rules — on any D20 Test. That is different from 2014, where it applied to attack rolls and death saves only.

**Q: I am at 0 hit points and take damage from a melee Critical Hit. What happens?**
A: Two death save failures — or instant death if the damage was at least your hit point maximum.

**Q: Does a healing spell from across the room get my ally up mid-fight?**
A: Yes. Any healing at 0 restores consciousness and wipes both death-save counters. They are still Prone.

**Q: Can my paladin cast a spell and then Divine Smite in the same turn?**
A: No. Divine Smite is a spell that expends a slot, and only one slot-expending spell may be cast per turn.

**Q: Two creatures grapple each other at once — legal?**
A: Yes. Grappled sets Speed to 0 but does not stop either of them acting. Both are stuck until one escapes or a grappler is Incapacitated.

**Q: If I Ready a spell and the trigger never happens, do I lose the slot?**
A: Yes. The spell is cast when you ready it. The slot is gone whether or not it goes off, and losing Concentration wastes it too.

**Q: My rogue hit with a Vex weapon. Do I have to do anything to get the Advantage?**
A: No. Mastery properties apply on their own when the hit lands. You just take the Advantage on your next attack against that creature.
